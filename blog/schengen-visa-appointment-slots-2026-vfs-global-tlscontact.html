Schengen Visa Appointment Slots in 2026: Why VFS Global and TLScontact Slots Have Disappeared — and What to Do

GV&J Strategy Team 20 Aug 2026 7 min read

Try booking a Schengen visa appointment in Lagos or Accra this month and you'll likely find the same thing: zero slots, for months, at every visa centre that matters. Applicants refresh VFS Global and TLScontact booking pages at 6am, watch a handful of openings appear, and lose them before the page even finishes loading. Some give up and pay a stranger on WhatsApp several hundred pounds for a slot that should have cost nothing. The Schengen visa appointment shortage isn't a rumour or a one-off bad week. It's the defining obstacle facing West African applicants right now, often before a single document gets checked.

Why Schengen Visa Appointment Slots Have Become So Scarce

Demand has simply outgrown capacity. Nigeria's rising middle class, the ongoing wave of professionals seeking opportunities abroad, and a general surge in outbound travel have pushed Schengen visa applications from Nigeria and Ghana to record volumes. VFS Global and TLScontact, the two firms most Schengen states use to outsource their application handling, haven't expanded biometric capture booths at anywhere near the same pace. Lagos alone processes applications for multiple Schengen countries through a handful of centres, and each biometric appointment takes a fixed slice of time that no amount of demand can compress.

The result is predictable. Wait times of four to ten weeks are now common for popular destinations like France, Spain and Germany. And the busiest windows book out even further ahead. Slots for June, July and August travel routinely fill during January and February. If you're planning a summer trip and only start looking for an appointment in April, you're already behind.

The Black Market Selling What Should Be Free

Here's what should trouble every applicant reading this. Appointment booking through VFS Global and TLScontact is free. Yet a thriving grey market has formed around it, particularly visible in Ghana, where unregulated agents openly guarantee a slot within days for a fee that can run into hundreds of cedis or pounds. Some of these operators use bots to scrape every new slot the instant it appears, locking legitimate applicants out entirely and then reselling access to the very system that's meant to be free.

We regularly see applicants who paid for a "guaranteed" appointment only to discover the booking details didn't match their passport, or that the slot vanished when they tried to confirm it. A pattern we observe consistently is desperation driving decisions that create new problems. Paying a third party to manipulate an official booking system isn't just risky. It can flag your file for additional scrutiny if a consulate later traces irregular booking activity back to your application.

How to Actually Secure a Schengen Visa Appointment

There's no trick that bypasses the shortage entirely, but there is a smarter way to work within it. Set alerts through the official VFS Global or TLScontact portal for your destination country and check at the exact times new slots typically release, usually early morning local time. Have every document ready to enter the booking form before you start, because hesitation costs you the slot to someone faster.

Consider your destination carefully too. Applicants fixed on Paris or Madrid face the tightest bottlenecks, while consulates for countries such as Portugal, Poland or the Nordic states sometimes have considerably shorter queues for equivalent Schengen access. Since a Schengen visa lets you travel across the entire area regardless of which country issued it, applying through a less congested consulate is a completely legitimate strategy, provided that country is genuinely your main destination or first point of entry, as the rules require.

A few practical habits make a measurable difference:

  • Check booking portals daily rather than weekly, ideally at consistent times
  • Prepare your document file completely before you start the booking process
  • Compare appointment availability across two or three Schengen states you could legitimately apply through
  • Avoid any agent or website that asks for payment to "guarantee" a slot

Timing Your Application Around the Slot Shortage

Booking early only helps if the rest of your file is ready to move with it. We see applicants secure a coveted appointment for March, then spend the following six weeks scrambling for bank statements, employer letters and proof of accommodation because they assumed they'd have more time. Treat the appointment date as your real deadline, not a soft target, and build your document collection backwards from it.

Give yourself a minimum of six to eight weeks before any date you actually need to travel. That buffer needs to absorb the appointment wait itself, standard processing time of roughly fifteen calendar days, and the possibility of a request for additional documents. Applicants chasing a fixed departure date under pressure make far more avoidable errors than those working with breathing room.

What to Do If You're Already Stuck Without a Slot

If every portal shows nothing available and your travel date is approaching, don't panic and don't pay a scalper. Widen your search to neighbouring application centres if your country has more than one, since Abuja and Port Harcourt sometimes show availability when Lagos doesn't. Check whether your intended destination country's consulate offers any premium or expedited service directly, which differs entirely from the black market resale schemes and carries no risk to your application's integrity.

And if none of that works in time, it may genuinely be better to push your trip back a few weeks than to rush a Schengen visa application filed in a panic. Files assembled under pressure are exactly the ones most likely to contain the inconsistencies that trigger a refusal, and a refusal costs you far more time than waiting for a proper slot ever would.

The Schengen visa appointment scramble is frustrating, but it's not insurmountable once you understand why it's happening and refuse to feed the black market that's profiting from it. Book early, keep your documents ready before you search, stay open to alternative consulates where genuinely appropriate, and treat your appointment date as the true start of your preparation deadline rather than the finish line. Getting a slot is only step one. What you submit once you're in the room is what actually decides your outcome.
