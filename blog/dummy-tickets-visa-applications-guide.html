Dummy Tickets for Visa Applications — What They Are and Why You Need One

GV&J Strategy Team 12 May 2026 4 min read

Every visa applicant eventually faces the same question: the embassy wants proof of a return flight, but I don't have my visa yet. Do I really need to buy a non-refundable £600 ticket I might lose if I'm refused?

The answer is no. The professional, legal, and globally accepted solution is a flight reservation — also known as a dummy ticket. Most applicants have never been told they exist.

What Is a Dummy Ticket?

A dummy ticket is a verifiable, real booking reference for a flight that is held but not paid in full. It's issued by a travel agent or specialist provider through the same airline reservation systems used by every legitimate booking. The reservation is valid for a limited time — typically 5 to 14 days — and contains:

  • A real Passenger Name Record (PNR) you can check on the airline's website
  • A complete itinerary with flight numbers, dates, times, and routes
  • Your name as it appears on your passport
  • Confirmation of seat hold

If the embassy attempts to verify the booking, it will check out as legitimate. The only thing absent is the final payment. Once your visa is approved, you either pay to convert the reservation into a real ticket, or you let it lapse and book a different flight that better suits your finalised plans.

Why Do Embassies Accept Them?

Embassies require proof of onward travel to confirm two things: your intention to leave at the end of your authorised stay, and that you have a realistic travel plan. A flight reservation proves both.

What embassies do not require is that you have paid for the ticket. They are visa-issuing authorities, not airlines. Their concern is whether your application is genuine, not whether you've yet committed thousands of pounds to a non-refundable booking.

This is why flight reservations are accepted by:

  • All 27 Schengen member states
  • The UK Standard Visitor Visa
  • The Canadian Temporary Resident Visa
  • The Australian eVisitor and Tourist Visa
  • Most other major destinations

The practice has been standard in the visa industry for over two decades.

The Financial Risk of Real Tickets

Here is the trap that catches thousands of applicants every year:

"I'll just buy the real ticket. It looks better, and I'll get the visa anyway, so what's the risk?"

The risk is significant. Schengen approval rates for genuinely well-prepared cases hover around 90–95%, depending on the consulate. That means even excellent applications face a 5–10% rejection rate. If you've spent £600 on a non-refundable flight before applying, and you are refused, you lose the entire amount.

Worse: refused applicants often try to rush a second application to use the booked dates. Rushed second applications are almost always weaker than the first. The result is a refusal on top of a refusal, plus the lost flight cost, plus the lost trip.

Never book non-refundable flights before you hold the physical visa sticker in your passport. This is the single most consistent rule we give every client.

How to Get a Legitimate Dummy Ticket

Not all dummy tickets are created equal. The market is unfortunately full of providers who issue fake PNRs that don't verify with the airline. If an embassy checks a forged reservation, you face an automatic refusal and a potential 10-year ban for document fraud.

A legitimate dummy ticket must:

  1. Be issued through a real GDS — Amadeus, Sabre, or Travelport — not invented on a template
  2. Have a verifiable PNR that the airline's website can confirm
  3. Be issued by a registered travel agency — not a Telegram seller or random WhatsApp contact
  4. Be valid for at least 7 days from issue
  5. Be in your exact passport name with correct route, dates, and class

A proper reservation costs between £20 and £60 depending on route complexity and validity period. Anyone offering a "dummy ticket" for £5 is selling you a forgery.

When You Need a Real Ticket Instead

There are a small number of situations where a real ticket is actually required:

  • Some embassies (Italy in particular) occasionally request a fully paid ticket for first-time applicants from certain nationalities
  • Once your visa is approved, the embassy may ask for proof of confirmed onward travel before you receive the physical visa
  • Border officers at entry sometimes ask to see the actual ticket, not the reservation

For these situations, you can always convert your reservation into a real ticket after approval. The wait time between approval and travel is usually sufficient to do this calmly without panic-booking.

The Right Order of Operations

This is the professional sequence used by every well-prepared applicant:

  1. Build your financial and documentation profile fully
  2. Obtain travel insurance that meets the Schengen €30,000 minimum
  3. Reserve your accommodation — refundable bookings until visa approved
  4. Get your flight reservation (dummy ticket) for the embassy file
  5. Submit your application
  6. Wait for the decision — do not book anything non-refundable
  7. After approval, pay for the real flight, finalise the hotel, get excited
