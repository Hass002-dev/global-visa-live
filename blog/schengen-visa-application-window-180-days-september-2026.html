Schengen Visa Application Window Now 180 Days: What the September 2026 Change Means for You

GV&J Strategy Team 7 September 2026 4 min read

What Changed on 1 September 2026

From 1 September 2026, the maximum period during which you can submit a Schengen visa application before your travel date doubled from 90 days to 180 days. This change applies across all Schengen member states and is now reflected in the guidance published by EU missions and visa processing centres.

Previously, the earliest you could submit your application and attend a biometric appointment was three months before your first day of intended travel. That constraint forced many applicants — especially those in markets with limited appointment slots — to gamble on booking travel before securing a visa, or to compress their document preparation into a short window.

The new 180-day rule changes both of those calculations.

Why This Matters More in West Africa

Appointment availability at VFS Global and TLScontact centres in Lagos, Accra, Banjul, and Dakar has tightened significantly through 2026. For summer travel, a pattern we observed consistently was applicants discovering in April or May that the earliest available appointment was already July or later — after their intended departure window.

With the window now at 180 days, you can legitimately book an appointment for travel six months away. The queue you join that far in advance is materially shorter than the one you will join in May or June, when demand spikes with school holiday plans.

For applicants in Nigeria and Ghana in particular, where Schengen appointment slots are routinely exhausted weeks or months ahead of peak periods, the 180-day window is not just a technicality — it is a practical tool for securing an appointment that was previously unavailable to you at all.

The Document Preparation Benefit

The extended window also gives you more time to build your supporting documents before your appointment, not after it.

Under the old 90-day rule, applicants applying as early as possible were often still waiting on payslips, bank statements covering the right number of months, or travel insurance that could not be purchased yet. Compressing three months of financial history into a short application window left gaps that consulate officers noticed.

With 180 days, you can:

  • Ensure your bank statement covers a full six-month period with consistent, explainable activity
  • Secure accommodation bookings that are genuinely refundable and credible for your travel dates
  • Obtain a flight reservation timed correctly for when your visa is likely to be issued
  • Prepare a thorough cover letter and itinerary without the pressure of an imminent appointment deadline

The consulate receives the same documentation either way — but a file built over six months of deliberate preparation reads differently from one assembled in a fortnight.

What the 180-Day Rule Does Not Change

It is worth being clear about what has not changed.

The validity of a Schengen visa is still determined by the consulate at point of issue. Applying 180 days in advance does not give you a longer visa — it simply moves the start of the application period earlier.

Biometric requirements at VFS Global and TLScontact remain the same. You still attend in person, provide fingerprints and a facial photograph, and submit your full document set at the appointment.

Consulate processing times have not been adjusted. Processing typically runs 15 calendar days, with some embassies taking 30 to 45 days for complex cases. Applying earlier means you receive your decision earlier — and have more time to respond if additional documents are requested or if you need to challenge a refusal.

The 90/180-day rule governing how long you can spend inside the Schengen Area once you arrive is entirely separate from this application window change. Your permitted days inside the Schengen Zone are unchanged.

How to Use the New Window

The practical implication is straightforward: move your application timeline forward by three months.

If you were previously planning to apply 10 to 12 weeks before travel, plan to apply 20 to 24 weeks before travel instead. Book the appointment at the earliest available slot — the further out you book, the more options you will have and the less likely you are to face a situation where the only available slot falls after your travel date.

If appointment availability in your city is already constrained, this change is your window to get significantly ahead of the queue before the next seasonal surge.
