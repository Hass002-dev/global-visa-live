Schengen Visa Fees in 2026: The Real Cost of Applying from Nigeria, Ghana, and the UK

Here's a figure worth sitting with before you book your biometrics appointment. Nearly half of all Schengen visa applications filed from Nigeria, Ghana, and Senegal were refused in the most recent EU statistics, and not a single one of those refused applicants got their money back. Schengen visa fees in 2026 now start at 90 euros per adult after the June increase, yet the amount that actually leaves your account is usually two to three times that figure once service charges, insurance, and courier costs are counted. If you're applying from Lagos, Accra, Banjul, Dakar, or London this year, you deserve to know exactly what you're paying for, and what happens to that money if the answer is no.

What Schengen Visa Fees in 2026 Actually Are

The headline number is straightforward. A short-stay Schengen visa now costs 90 euros for adults across all 29 member and associated states, following the increase that took effect in June 2026. Children aged six to eleven pay 45 euros, and children under six apply free of charge. Certain categories, including some students and researchers travelling for study purposes, may qualify for reduced or waived fees, though in practice we see very few West African applicants who fall into an exempt category.

That 90 euros is set by EU regulation, reviewed every three years, and it is identical whether you apply through France, Spain, Italy, or the Netherlands. No consulate offers a discount, and any agent who claims they can get you a cheaper embassy fee is charging you for something that doesn't exist.

The Hidden Charges Behind Schengen Visa Fees in 2026

The embassy fee is only the entry ticket. Most Schengen states outsource their visa handling to VFS Global or TLS Contact, and the service charge for lodging your file typically adds the equivalent of 30 to 45 euros depending on the country and location. On top of that sit the optional extras these centres actively sell: SMS tracking, premium lounges, photocopying, photographs, courier return of your passport, and prime-time appointment slots.

Some of these extras are genuinely useful. Courier return matters if you live far from the application centre, and applicants in cities like Kano or Tamale often save more in transport than the courier costs. But many of the add-ons are padding, and a pattern we observe consistently is applicants spending 60 to 100 euros on centre extras that have zero influence on the decision.

Then come the costs nobody lists on the fee schedule. Travel medical insurance with 30,000 euros of cover is mandatory and typically costs 15 to 40 euros for a short trip. Flight reservations, hotel bookings, document translations, and notarisations all add up. For a family of four applying from Nigeria, the true Schengen visa cost routinely lands between 700 and 1,000 euros before anyone has boarded a plane.

Why a Refusal Makes Everything More Expensive

Every euro described above is non-refundable. The embassy keeps the 90 euros whether your visa is granted or refused, and VFS or TLS keeps its service charge too. And that's where the refusal statistics stop being abstract. With refusal rates for Nigerian, Ghanaian, and Senegalese applicants sitting between roughly 41 and 47 percent in recent EU data, close to half of the money paid in application fees from these countries buys nothing but a refusal letter.

Refused applicants then face the same bill again on reapplication. Many of the files we review show applicants on their second or third attempt who have spent 400 euros or more in cumulative fees, often repeating the same fixable documentation error each time. The most expensive part of a Schengen application isn't the fee schedule. It's paying that schedule twice.

Does the New ETIAS Fee Affect You?

There's been real confusion in West African communities about the ETIAS fee, so let's settle it. ETIAS is the 20 euro travel authorisation launching in the final quarter of 2026, and it applies only to visa-exempt nationals such as British, American, and Canadian passport holders. If you hold a Nigerian, Ghanaian, Gambian, or Senegalese passport, ETIAS does not replace your visa and you won't pay the 20 euros. You'll continue applying for a standard Schengen visa at 90 euros.

UK residents holding British passports are the ones who should prepare for ETIAS. One warning applies to everyone though: the official portal hasn't opened yet, and every website currently selling ETIAS applications is fraudulent. The only legitimate address will sit on the europa.eu domain.

How to Budget So You Only Pay Once

Treat the visa budget as two separate pots. Pot one is the unavoidable core: the 90 euro embassy fee, the service charge, and compliant insurance. Pot two is everything optional, and each item in it should earn its place. Skip the premium lounge. Keep the courier if distance justifies it.

Timing also affects cost. Applicants who book early pay standard rates for insurance and reservations, while those rushing two weeks before travel pay premium prices for everything and often accept whichever appointment slot appears, sometimes in another city entirely. We regularly see applicants from Ghana and The Gambia spend more on last-minute domestic travel to reach a biometrics appointment than on the visa fee itself. Applying eight to twelve weeks ahead of your trip keeps every cost in the ordinary range and leaves room to fix problems calmly.

The smarter investment sits before submission, not inside the application centre. A refusal caused by an inconsistent bank statement, a weak employment letter, or a mismatched itinerary costs you the entire pot one, plus weeks of delay, plus a refusal record that consular officers will see on your next attempt. Applicants who spend modestly on getting the file right the first time consistently spend less overall than those who budget only for fees and hope.

Money spent on a refused application is gone. That's the single most important fact about Schengen visa fees in 2026, and it should shape how you prepare. Get the file right before the fee leaves your account, and the 90 euros becomes the price of a decision in your favour rather than a donation to a consulate.
