The Gap in the Middle

An industry split into two extremes.

Regulated, expensive Cheap, dangerous
Solicitors & IAA-registered advisers
The gap Global Visa & Journeys
Unregulated fixers

On one side sit immigration solicitors and IAA-registered advisers. They are the right people for an appeal, a human rights case, settlement, or anything where the law itself is in dispute, and they charge accordingly. But a teacher in Banjul applying for a ten-day trip to Spain has no legal dispute. She has a documents problem: a bank statement that raises a question nobody told her to answer.

On the other side sit the unregulated fixers. They promise what nobody can promise, and some deliver it by inventing the evidence. The client often does not know. When a consulate spots it, the refusal is not a simple no — the reason is recorded in a system other member states can see.

Between the two is the person who is genuinely eligible and simply presenting badly. Nobody was serving them. That is the gap GVJ was built for.

How It Works

The Corridor.

Every case we handle moves along the same route: from a document in Banjul, Lagos or Accra, to a decision made in Europe.

  1. Documents

    We forensically review every document before it's gathered — bank statements, employer letters, proof of ties — so nothing a visa officer could doubt reaches the file.

  2. Submission

    We prepare the file and book the appointment. From there, the counter has two sides.

    Applicant's side

    You attend in person, present your documents, and go through biometrics.

    Consulate's side

    A visa officer checks the same file against a checklist most applicants never see — consistency, sufficiency, intent to return.

  3. Decision

    We track the case through to the embassy's decision and explain exactly what it means, whichever way it goes.

  4. Travel

    Once the visa is issued, the journey itself is logistics — flights, insurance, and the practical detail of getting there.

Who We Are

Both sides of the counter.

I didn't start this firm by reading about the visa industry. I started it by living on both ends of the corridor.

I've sat at a kitchen table in Banjul, helping gather a bank statement that was about to raise the wrong question — long before I had a name for it. And I've sat on the UK side, where that same file lands, and understood exactly what a visa officer is trained to check and doubt.

Most firms in this industry know one end. A solicitor knows the law. A fixer knows what sells. I don't think either has stood on both sides of that counter at once — and that is the gap Global Visa & Journeys was built to close.
