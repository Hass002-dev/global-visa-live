How to Apply for a Schengen Visa from the UK

Start Here

Do You Need a Schengen Visa From the UK?

If you hold a British passport, you don't need a Schengen visa for a short trip — you can already travel visa-free. From late 2026 you'll instead need ETIAS, a separate, cheaper travel authorisation, not a visa. If that's you, this page isn't for you, and that's good news.

If you're living in the UK on a work, study, family, or settled-status visa, a BRP, or UK eVisa status, and your own passport is not British, you almost certainly need a Schengen visa before travelling to any of the 29 Schengen area countries for tourism or business. This page covers exactly how that application works, applying from inside the UK.

Jurisdiction

Which Country Do You Apply To?

Your Schengen visa application goes to the embassy or visa centre of your main destination — the country where you're spending the most nights across your whole trip, not the country you land in first. If you fly into Paris but spend 8 of your 10 nights in Spain, you apply through the Spanish route, not the French one.

If your nights are genuinely split equally between two or more countries, the rule shifts to whichever country you enter the Schengen area through first. Getting this wrong isn't a minor slip — applying to the wrong country's visa centre can mean an administrative refusal before anyone has even reviewed your documents, and that refusal is now recorded and visible to every consulate you apply to afterwards. Visa Centres

Where You Apply in the UK

You don't apply at an embassy counter. Each Schengen country outsources UK applications to a visa centre, and which one you use — and whether you can pick your own appointment date — depends on the country:

Fees

The Real Cost of a Schengen Visa From the UK

The headline embassy fee is EUR 90 per adult, set by EU regulation and identical whichever Schengen country you apply through. Children aged 6 to 11 typically pay a reduced fee (around EUR 45, or free in some countries), and children under 6 apply free. In GBP terms this converts to roughly £75–£81 depending on the country and the exchange rate on the day.

On top of that sits the visa centre's own service fee, which does vary by country and currently ranges from around £15 to £35 based on the centres' own published rates (BLS, TLScontact and GVCW each set their own charge). Mandatory travel insurance meeting the EUR 30,000 minimum typically adds another €15–€40. Optional extras — courier passport return, premium appointment slots, SMS tracking — are sold by some centres and are never required to get a visa; skip anything that doesn't genuinely solve a real problem for you.

Every one of these fees is non-refundable, win or lose. The exact current fee and service charge for your country is on that country's own page below.

Timing

How Long It Takes, and When to Apply

You can submit a Schengen visa application up to six months before your departure date — and during busy periods, you should be using nearly all of that window, not waiting until your plans feel finalised.

Once you've submitted, standard processing is 15 calendar days, though it can legally stretch to 45 days if a consulate wants further checks — this happens more often than applicants expect. Before you even get to that stage, getting the appointment itself typically takes four to ten weeks, depending on the country and the season, with the busiest summer windows booking out months in advance.

Add it together and a realistic timeline from "I want to apply" to "I have a decision" runs two to three months for a popular destination in peak season. Not sure where to start counting backwards from your travel date? Try our visa timeline planner.

Appointments

Why Appointments Are Hard to Get

Demand for Schengen appointments has outgrown the visa centres' capacity, and booking slots is free — which is exactly why a grey market of agents has formed around it, offering a "guaranteed" slot for a fee. Booking through one is risky: some of these operators scrape every new slot the moment it appears using bots, and applicants who pay them have ended up with details that don't match their passport, or a slot that simply vanishes when they try to confirm it.

For the countries where you choose your own slot (Spain, Greece, Belgium), new availability tends to release at consistent times, often early morning. Have your documents ready before you start searching, since hesitation costs you the slot to someone faster. For France and Germany, you're placed in a queue and allocated an appointment automatically — there's no slot-hunting to do, just registering and waiting.

Never pay anyone to "guarantee" an appointment. It's against the centres' own terms, and we regularly see applicants lose both the money and the slot.

Common Mistakes

Why UK Residents Get Refused

These are the patterns that come up again and again in applications from UK residents with a non-British passport:

  1. Assuming a valid UK status speaks for itself. A consulate officer doesn't see a UK eVisa and conclude you're low-risk. They see a third-country national who still has to independently prove strong ties, financial stability, and genuine intent to return.
  2. Applying to the wrong country. Choosing whichever consulate seems fastest or most lenient, rather than your genuine main destination, is grounds for an administrative refusal that's now recorded and visible to every consulate afterwards.
  3. A bank statement that doesn't tell a consistent story. Large unexplained deposits shortly before the appointment, transfers from third parties, or a balance that doesn't match your stated income all invite closer scrutiny — officers read the pattern, not just the total.
  4. Inadequate travel insurance. A policy with narrow geographic coverage, one that excludes pre-existing conditions with no alternative cover, or a single-country policy submitted for a multi-country trip gets flagged at document review.
  5. An itinerary that's really just a hotel booking. Officers want to see day-by-day planning — transport between cities, bookings, a clear explanation of what you're doing and why — not a single confirmation email standing in for a plan.
  6. No evidence tying you to the UK. Solid income and a clean travel history aren't enough on their own if nothing in the file roots you here — a tenancy agreement, dependants, or your employer's confirmed return-to-work date all do that job.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply for a Schengen visa in the UK?

Yes, if you hold a non-British passport and are living in the UK on a visa, BRP, or settled status. British passport holders do not need a Schengen visa for short stays. You apply at a visa centre in the UK — VFS Global, TLScontact, or BLS International, depending on the country — not at an embassy counter.

How much is a Schengen visa from the UK?

The embassy fee is EUR 90 for adults (reduced or free for children, depending on age and country), plus a visa centre service fee that varies by country — roughly £15 to £35 based on current published rates. Add mandatory travel insurance and any optional extras like courier passport return.

Which country should I apply to?

The country where you'll spend the most nights on your trip — your main destination. If your nights are split exactly evenly between two or more countries, you apply to the country you enter first. Applying to the wrong country can mean an administrative refusal, regardless of how strong your documents are.

How long does it take?

Standard processing is 15 calendar days after your appointment, though it can legally extend to 45 days if a consulate wants further checks. On top of that, getting an appointment itself can take four to ten weeks depending on the country and season. You can apply up to six months before you travel, and during busy periods you should use most of that window.
